Rules

  1. One basket or rolling pin fits in each worker's hands. Nearest accessible pickup uses distance then object ID; lids must be open for tier pickup.
  2. Roll raw baskets on the counter while holding the pin. Vegetable needs one roll, dense two; completed preparation consumes one clean wrapper.
  3. Two physical tiers share pressure. Any open lid subtracts two pressure per tick; a fueled wet burner adds one up to three.
  4. Closed tiers cook only with pressure at least two and boiler water one through four. Vegetable accepts ticks four to six, dense seven to nine.
  5. The burner consumes finite fuel. Every third heating tick evaporates one water; three dry-heating danger ends the shift.
  6. Water above four wets loaded baskets, and overflow wets them further. Moisture three spoils a prepared or ready batch.
  7. Serve at the hatch; the basket becomes dirty. Wash at the sink using one flask unit, or discard a bad batch there.
  8. Actions and state are identical for human and policy roles; scripts are unexecuted source hypotheses.

Steam Basket Kitchen

Three workers use seventeen native actions on an eleven-by-seven kitchen. Two tiers have separate real positions but one boiler. Three basket identities, a portable rolling pin, one rolling counter, a pantry, sink and service hatch constrain space and scheduling. Workers can transfer items by putting them down for a peer; there is no invisible dispatch or kitchen planner.

Movement checks bounds, the counter partition and old worker occupancy. Rotating priority resolves contested destinations and no swaps/following occur. Invalid or masked requests become wait. Interactions run in rotating actor order with capacity and supply rechecks. Pickup chooses the nearest accessible basket/pin, with object ID breaking ties; a closed tier is physically inaccessible. A carried basket can be put at the worker's cell if no loose basket occupies it. A pin can share a station cell. Loading a tier requires a carried prepared basket, distance one, an open lid and no other attached basket.

Initial upper/lower baskets are prepared vegetable/dense batches with one cooked tick each. A raw vegetable batch is on the rolling counter, and the pin is under the porter. The pantry has one vegetable and two dense portions, so total portions are three per filling. Four additional clean wrappers remain after the two initial prepared batches. Filling requires an empty carried basket at the pantry; one rolling action prepares vegetable, two prepare dense, using the common pin beside a raw basket on the counter. Completion consumes a wrapper. Filled baskets retain the filling worker as owner.

A nearby action toggles one tier. Any open lid vents the shared boiler by two each tick. A burning fueled boiler with positive water adds one pressure, otherwise pressure falls one, with [0,3] clipping. Burner fuel begins at twenty-four and decreases once per heating tick; exhausted fuel turns the burner off. Closing one tier cannot preserve its cooking rate when the other is open. Only an attached closed tier at pressure at least two and boiler water one through four advances cook by one. Vegetable becomes ready at four and spoils above six; dense becomes ready at seven and spoils above nine. Removing a ready basket stops its clock.

Pouring empties the actor's flask into the boiler up to six and records excess as spilled water. A heating boiler above four increases loaded-basket moisture by one; overflow adds two more. Moisture is persistent and spoils prepared/ready baskets at three. Every third heating transition evaporates one water. Heating dry increases danger; other ticks reduce it by one, clipped to three. Thus opening a tier, stopping the burner, adding water, or fetching and cleaning a basket each has different consequences for another cook's timing and safety. These are source-defined alternatives, not a performance claim.

Serving a ready basket at the hatch pays every role one, the server 0.2 and batch owner 0.2. It empties the batch fields but leaves a carried dirty basket. Washing at the sink spends one carried water unit; washing a nonempty batch increments waste and costs the actor 0.1. Sink stock supplies one refill per action up to flask capacity two. Accepted non-wait actions cost 0.01; joint failure costs one. For each filling, initial three portions equal pantry plus served plus waste plus baskets still carrying that filling, including spoiled baskets. Basket/pin identities persist, and only one basket attaches to each tier. Water is finite; water_lost specifically counts boiler evaporation/overflow, not washing consumption.

Success requires two served of each filling. Failure follows actual dry-heating danger or a recipe ledger with fewer than two accepted plus pantry plus unspoiled active portions. The scripts request rolling plus upper-basket retrieval, and a deliberate burner pause with water replenishment. The paired hypothesis changes only the upper cook's lid action after one wait; three suffix ticks expose both immediate access and delayed pressure effects on the lower basket. No service success or executed cooking window is claimed.
