# GI210 · Lantern Grid

Transport and sort six finite ore blocks, then carry them to the west depot while managing a finite electric grid.

## Objective and gameplay

Transport and sort six finite ore blocks, then carry them to the west depot while managing a finite electric grid.

Toggling a branch allocates shared capacitor charge; demand above trunk capacity trips the highest-demand branch. Lamp power changes other crews' visible objects, while lift and sorter power alter transport and processing opportunities.

- Three crews share one finite electric grid and six ore blocks. All roles use the same native actions.

- Lamp, lift and sorter draw 1, 3 and 2 units per tick. The common trunk carries at most 4.

- Excess demand trips the highest-demand branch fuse before supply. A carried spare fuse repairs it.

- A generator crank spends one of nine fuel units to add six charge when charge is at most six.

- Wire the lift using a carried cable, or hold its manual bypass for three ticks. Movement uses the previous interval's power.

- Powered light expands nearby crews' sight from one to four tiles; hidden items remain hidden in role view.

- Sort carried ore beside the powered sorter, then take it to the west depot. Materials are never respawned.

- All six deposits finish naturally; tick 160 otherwise truncates. This source and its scripts are unexecuted.

Ending: Natural completion when all six ore blocks have been delivered. Otherwise truncate at tick 160.

## What this game explores

Resource commons & externalities: An action changes a persistent resource or burden that another role also encounters. Compare extraction with maintenance and record the downstream resource trajectory.

Complementary production & service: A useful outcome requires ordered stages, scarce equipment or another role's capability. Measure real completed products and resource circulation rather than counting intermediate actions as success.

Joint transport & shared access: Positions, load geometry, occupancy and persistent access constrain whose action can succeed next. Distinguish physical handoff, shared controls and simultaneous force from a cosmetic cooperation label.

These are questions supported by rule structure, not observed learning effects.

## Reinforcement-learning integration

```python
import jax
from crucibles_jaxmarl import make

env = make("gi210")
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
key, rk, ak, sk = jax.random.split(key, 4)
obs, state = jax.jit(env.reset)(rk)
actions = env.sample_actions(ak, state)
obs, state, rewards, dones, info = jax.jit(env.step_env)(sk, state, actions)
done = dones["__all__"]
```

Observations are float32 vectors of width 167; actions are scalar int32; masks have 14 entries. Supply all 3 roles each transition. Masked sampling is an engineering reference, not a trained learner.

`step_env` retains terminal data without reset. `step` performs outer autoreset and keeps terminal data in `info["raw_state"]` and `info["raw_observation"]`. Use `dones["__all__"]` for episode boundaries; separate completion, failure and time limit in analysis.

JIT or vmap equal identities and shapes. Split independent reset, policy and transition keys. Group different shapes explicitly; do not shrink the population or horizon. Host rendering stays outside training.
