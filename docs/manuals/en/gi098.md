## Objective and gameplay

Charge all three receivers to three. Each role gains more from its own receiver; launch credit and the shared completion bonus create additional motives to assist.

A shared movable-worker optical board. Adjacent workers turn or clamp the same mirrors, spending common energy. Pulses take one cell per tick, reflect at actual mirror cells, and are consumed by receivers or board exit/TTL. A partner can reroute a pulse while it travels, deny a turn by clamping, or restore energy at a distant pump.

- Three workers share seven mirrors, one emitter and one rechargeable energy pool.

- Move to a mirror to rotate it, or lock its current direction while a pulse is travelling.

- The emitter launches one eastward pulse at a time. It travels one real cell each tick, for at most forty ticks.

- Transparent mirrors pass straight; slash and backslash mirrors reflect using the displayed direction.

- Every turn or clamp costs one shared energy; a launch costs three. The pump restores up to two.

- Each receiver needs three pulses. Private receiver rewards, launch credit and joint completion reward coexist.

Ending: All receiver charges equal three. Otherwise truncate at 96 nonterminal steps. Natural success takes precedence on the horizon; terminal raw calls are absorbing and reward zero.

## What this game explores

Resource commons & externalities: An action changes a persistent resource or burden that another role also encounters. Compare extraction with maintenance and record the downstream resource trajectory.

Complementary production & service: A useful outcome requires ordered stages, scarce equipment or another role's capability. Measure real completed products and resource circulation rather than counting intermediate actions as success.

Joint transport & shared access: Positions, load geometry, occupancy and persistent access constrain whose action can succeed next. Distinguish physical handoff, shared controls and simultaneous force from a cosmetic cooperation label.

These are questions supported by rule structure, not observed learning effects.

## Reinforcement-learning integration

```python
import jax
from crucibles_jaxmarl import make

env = make("gi098")
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
key, rk, ak, sk = jax.random.split(key, 4)
obs, state = jax.jit(env.reset)(rk)
actions = env.sample_actions(ak, state)
obs, state, rewards, dones, info = jax.jit(env.step_env)(sk, state, actions)
done = dones["__all__"]
```

Observations are float32 vectors of width 73; actions are scalar int32; masks have 9 entries. Supply all 3 roles each transition. Masked sampling is an engineering reference, not a trained learner.

`step_env` retains terminal data without reset. `step` performs outer autoreset and keeps terminal data in `info["raw_state"]` and `info["raw_observation"]`. Use `dones["__all__"]` for episode boundaries; separate completion, failure and time limit in analysis.

JIT or vmap equal identities and shapes. Split independent reset, policy and transition keys. Group different shapes explicitly; do not shrink the population or horizon. Host rendering stays outside training.
