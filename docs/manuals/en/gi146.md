# GI146 · Firebreak Pumps and Shared Evacuation

Evacuate both occupants at the refuge before either suffers fatal heat exposure; share the finite water supply.

## Objective and gameplay

Evacuate both occupants at the refuge before either suffers fatal heat exposure; share the finite water supply.

Concurrent pump/receive and give/receive transfer real water. Ground couplings transmit pressure; picking one up can sever another firefighter's supply. A water-consuming console holder opens the gate for a different mover in that transition. Sprays alter later travel and occupant safety.

- Three firefighters share 24 total water units: four initially carried and twenty in the pump. Tanks hold four.

- Ground hose couplings conduct only over Manhattan-one links to the pump. A carried coupling does not conduct.

- Pump and receive must occur together. Each receiver gets at most one pump unit per turn, and pump throughput is two.

- Giving transfers one water to an adjacent consenting receiver. Spraying and holding the console each consume one water.

- The console opens the firebreak only for the current joint transition. A displayed open flag describes the preceding transition.

- Moves into occupied old cells and contested destinations fail; swaps and follow-through are disallowed.

- Sprays clear radius-one fire. Every eighth transition fire spreads one edge using finite fuel.

- Carry one occupant at a time to the refuge. Any casualty ends the rescue; two evacuations succeed. Horizon is eighty.

Ending: Success when both occupants have status one; failure as soon as any occupant has status two. Otherwise the 80-transition horizon truncates. Natural completion takes priority at the horizon.

## What this game explores

Resource commons & externalities: An action changes a persistent resource or burden that another role also encounters. Compare extraction with maintenance and record the downstream resource trajectory.

Survival, protection & rescue: Protection and repair alter another role's future feasible actions. Record losses, exposure, rescue and infrastructure as well as delivery; survival is determined by the actual kernel.

These are questions supported by rule structure, not observed learning effects.

## Reinforcement-learning integration

```python
import jax
from crucibles_jaxmarl import make

env = make("gi146")
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
key, rk, ak, sk = jax.random.split(key, 4)
obs, state = jax.jit(env.reset)(rk)
actions = env.sample_actions(ak, state)
obs, state, rewards, dones, info = jax.jit(env.step_env)(sk, state, actions)
done = dones["__all__"]
```

Observations are float32 vectors of width 304; actions are scalar int32; masks have 13 entries. Supply all 3 roles each transition. Masked sampling is an engineering reference, not a trained learner.

`step_env` retains terminal data without reset. `step` performs outer autoreset and keeps terminal data in `info["raw_state"]` and `info["raw_observation"]`. Use `dones["__all__"]` for episode boundaries; separate completion, failure and time limit in analysis.

JIT or vmap equal identities and shapes. Split independent reset, policy and transition keys. Group different shapes explicitly; do not shrink the population or horizon. Host rendering stays outside training.
