## Objective and gameplay

Each controlled keeper values its own harvests and must balance its water supply with shared flood prevention and downstream access.

Mobile operators toggle real connected basin gates. Pre-flux outgoing water crosses one edge per tick; downstream irrigation therefore depends on earlier upstream choices. Finite repair kits can be given to adjacent peers.

- Move on land; keepers may co-locate. Waterway cells are impassable. Operate a gate from Manhattan distance at most one.

- Each toggle targets the lowest-index reachable gate; simultaneous operators toggle it only when their count is odd.

- Every fourth tick adds two units upstream. Open gates send up to two pre-flux units to the next basin; gate 2 drains the outlet. Incoming water cannot cascade in the same tick.

- Capacity is six plus bank reinforcement. Overflow loses excess water, damages plot integrity and sets a three-tick inundation timer.

- Near a plot, spend a kit to add bank capacity up to two, then repair integrity. Gifts send one kit to a nearby peer with space.

- Irrigate your own adjacent plot using one unit. Three accepted irrigations yield one privately valued harvest; two harvests per plot complete the task.

- Any destroyed plot ends the episode. Private values are excluded from peers and scene attributes.

Ending: All three plots reach two harvests, or any plot integrity becomes zero. Natural completion has priority at H=96.

## What this game explores

Resource commons & externalities: An action changes a persistent resource or burden that another role also encounters. Compare extraction with maintenance and record the downstream resource trajectory.

Complementary production & service: A useful outcome requires ordered stages, scarce equipment or another role's capability. Measure real completed products and resource circulation rather than counting intermediate actions as success.

Joint transport & shared access: Positions, load geometry, occupancy and persistent access constrain whose action can succeed next. Distinguish physical handoff, shared controls and simultaneous force from a cosmetic cooperation label.

These are questions supported by rule structure, not observed learning effects.

## Reinforcement-learning integration

```python
import jax
from crucibles_jaxmarl import make

env = make("gi174")
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
key, rk, ak, sk = jax.random.split(key, 4)
obs, state = jax.jit(env.reset)(rk)
actions = env.sample_actions(ak, state)
obs, state, rewards, dones, info = jax.jit(env.step_env)(sk, state, actions)
done = dones["__all__"]
```

Observations are float32 vectors of width 59; actions are scalar int32; masks have 9 entries. Supply all 3 roles each transition. Masked sampling is an engineering reference, not a trained learner.

`step_env` retains terminal data without reset. `step` performs outer autoreset and keeps terminal data in `info["raw_state"]` and `info["raw_observation"]`. Use `dones["__all__"]` for episode boundaries; separate completion, failure and time limit in analysis.

JIT or vmap equal identities and shapes. Split independent reset, policy and transition keys. Group different shapes explicitly; do not shrink the population or horizon. Host rendering stays outside training.
