## Objective and gameplay

Use six finite parts to satisfy three chassis-plus-coil orders, reworking defects and matching requested coil colors, then physically carry valid products to the dock before their role-linked deadlines when possible.

Routing, braking and occupied conveyor/machine buffers change partners available parts and delays. Inspection or recipe publication changes partner information. Shared power spent on one rework may prevent a different repair. Assembly permanently consumes two finite parts, and poor hidden-quality decisions impose shared losses.

- The conveyor advances on even live ticks. Each buffer holds one uncarried active part; current occupancy blocks entry and contention uses cyclic part priority.

- The central junction routes straight to the assembly bench, upward into one machine, or downward into the other. Braking freezes belt motion while deadlines continue.

- A worker carries at most one part or product. Cyclic role priority resolves contested pickups, drops, machine starts and product collection.

- Rework consumes two shared power and three subsequent processing ticks to clear a defect. Color flip consumes one power and two subsequent ticks.

- Role 1 sees defects and role 2 sees recipes initially. Inspection publishes a part defect; using a nearby order board publishes the recipes.

- Assembly consumes the lowest-index available chassis and coil near the bench for the first pending order. Defective or wrong-color pairs are permanently rejected; valid products must be carried to the dock.

Ending: All three product statuses are resolved as shipped (2) or rejected (3). Rejected orders are permanent negative outcomes. Ready but undelivered products do not count as completion. Otherwise truncate at 144 live transitions.

Action timing: Every indexed intent is legal for a live capable role, even when its spatial/resource precondition fails; failed intents become deterministic no-ops. Out-of-range actions become wait. Terminal roles, and temporarily/incapacitated roles where specified, retain wait only. Masks do not leak private feasibility.

## What this game explores

Complementary production & service: A useful outcome requires ordered stages, scarce equipment or another role's capability. Measure real completed products and resource circulation rather than counting intermediate actions as success.

Joint transport & shared access: Positions, load geometry, occupancy and persistent access constrain whose action can succeed next. Distinguish physical handoff, shared controls and simultaneous force from a cosmetic cooperation label.

Team opposition & interception: Cooperation within a team can coexist with opposition between teams. Preserve team membership, capture and possession rules, and report each team's outcomes instead of assuming a single shared return.

These are questions supported by rule structure, not observed learning effects.

## Reinforcement-learning integration

```python
import jax
from crucibles_jaxmarl import make

env = make("gi133")
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
key, rk, ak, sk = jax.random.split(key, 4)
obs, state = jax.jit(env.reset)(rk)
actions = env.sample_actions(ak, state)
obs, state, rewards, dones, info = jax.jit(env.step_env)(sk, state, actions)
done = dones["__all__"]
```

Observations are float32 vectors of width 303; actions are scalar int32; masks have 13 entries. Supply all 4 roles each transition. Masked sampling is an engineering reference, not a trained learner.

`step_env` retains terminal data without reset. `step` performs outer autoreset and keeps terminal data in `info["raw_state"]` and `info["raw_observation"]`. Use `dones["__all__"]` for episode boundaries; separate completion, failure and time limit in analysis.

JIT or vmap equal identities and shapes. Split independent reset, policy and transition keys. Group different shapes explicitly; do not shrink the population or horizon. Host rendering stays outside training.
