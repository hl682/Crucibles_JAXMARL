## Objective and gameplay

Keep all three crew warm, manufacture three plates and two gaskets, install them into the rescue beacon, and operate it with at least two adjacent crew for three consecutive effective charge steps while the shared hearth has fuel.

Finite spatial deposits, six-unit bags, shared recipe buffers, cooperative labor, handoffs, shared hearth fuel, optional collective insulation investment, and two-person beacon operation create direct dependencies in resources, opportunities and timing.

- Move on ice paths; deposits and workshop cells are solid. Conflicting movements use a rotating crew priority and never swap occupied cells.

- Harvest finite ore, reeds and timber. Each specialist gathers two units of its material; everyone else gathers one. Bags hold six total items.

- Load two ore plus one timber into the foundry for a plate, or two reeds plus one timber into the loom for a gasket. Four units of labor finish either recipe.

- The weaver supplies double loom labor; the engineer supplies double foundry labor. Loaded ingredients and unfinished labor persist for other crew to use.

- Loading timber into the hearth supplies twelve fuel turns. Nearby crew warm while fuel burns; rest adds more warmth, and timber can also warm neighbors as a field fire.

- One plate plus one gasket can instead buy a shelter insulation level. This expands the heated radius and lowers later cold loss, delaying rescue assembly.

- Load three plates and two gaskets into the beacon. At least two adjacent crew must operate it together for three charge steps with hearth fuel; charge leaks when cooperation breaks.

- If any crew freezes, the joint mission fails. Natural success or failure precedes the 192-step timeout.

Ending: Success when beacon charge reaches three and all warmth values remain positive; failure when any crew reaches zero warmth. Otherwise truncate at 192. Natural termination takes priority on the horizon step. Terminal raw calls preserve all state and clock and return zero reward.

## What this game explores

Resource commons & externalities: An action changes a persistent resource or burden that another role also encounters. Compare extraction with maintenance and record the downstream resource trajectory.

Complementary production & service: A useful outcome requires ordered stages, scarce equipment or another role's capability. Measure real completed products and resource circulation rather than counting intermediate actions as success.

Survival, protection & rescue: Protection and repair alter another role's future feasible actions. Record losses, exposure, rescue and infrastructure as well as delivery; survival is determined by the actual kernel.

These are questions supported by rule structure, not observed learning effects.

## Reinforcement-learning integration

```python
import jax
from crucibles_jaxmarl import make

env = make("gi078")
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
key, rk, ak, sk = jax.random.split(key, 4)
obs, state = jax.jit(env.reset)(rk)
actions = env.sample_actions(ak, state)
obs, state, rewards, dones, info = jax.jit(env.step_env)(sk, state, actions)
done = dones["__all__"]
```

Observations are float32 vectors of width 209; actions are scalar int32; masks have 12 entries. Supply all 3 roles each transition. Masked sampling is an engineering reference, not a trained learner.

`step_env` retains terminal data without reset. `step` performs outer autoreset and keeps terminal data in `info["raw_state"]` and `info["raw_observation"]`. Use `dones["__all__"]` for episode boundaries; separate completion, failure and time limit in analysis.

JIT or vmap equal identities and shapes. Split independent reset, policy and transition keys. Group different shapes explicitly; do not shrink the population or horizon. Host rendering stays outside training.
