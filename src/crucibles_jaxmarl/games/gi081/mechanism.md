# Dockside Promises — physical logistics with binding commitments

AUTHORED_NOT_EXECUTED. Four robots share a 13 by 9 dock, an exclusive central corridor and two tidal bypass crossings. Each courier has two actual parcel orders. There are 11 homogeneous actions, 254 float32 observation columns and horizon 128. This is an original traffic/reservation transition system, not a pallet-hauled switch route. All signed integer world quantities use int32. Deterministic rules accept the supplied key without generating or storing another key.

## Direct cross-agent dependencies

A right-of-way request places one actual service credit into a bond, states eastward or westward intent, and persists in a ten-step queue. An eligible oldest request receives an eight-step central-corridor lease, with rotating tie priority. Eligibility requires age four or an active yield pledge on the opposite bank. A partner can therefore shorten the wait by yielding, but that pledge binds the partner to stay out of the corridor for six steps and relinquishes its own current/pending claim without refund. Request, pledge and lease are public state; communication is consequential action, not a detached text channel.

The lessee must physically enter corridor cells (5,4), (6,4) or (7,4) and exit on its requested opposite side before completion to recover the bond. Expiration or abandoning a claim forfeits it. A qualifying opposite-side yielder receives at most one credit per pledge, only when the partner actually completes that crossing while the pledge timer remains positive. It is possible to yield without receiving compensation if the partner fails, delays or takes another route. No real money or external payment is involved.

Who changes whose feasible actions: an active lease prevents other robots entering the protected corridor. Actual robot occupancy can still block the lessee's exit or approach. A pledge changes both the queue's admission test and the pledger's own future masks. Existing corridor occupants can clear after lease expiry, but a new lease is withheld until the corridor is empty. An opponent or uncoordinated partner can therefore delay others even without owning a route. North/south tidal bridges offer temporally varying alternative paths instead of a single mandatory bottleneck.

Who changes whose resources and returns: a courier can spend two of its own battery units to give an adjacent low-energy robot two units. The receiver gains actual future movement options; the donor loses them. A charger can serve only one neighboring robot per step, so access and rotating contention matter. Ordinary movement rejects occupied cells, but two adjacent robots both choosing action 10 can swap outside the central corridor at a cost of two energy each. This gives an explicit, costly consent protocol for resolving local obstruction rather than silently allowing all head-on swaps.

Concrete partner-dependent response: a loaded west courier at the central entrance can request and plan to cross promptly if an east courier yields. If that courier instead keeps its own queued claim and occupies the exit, the west courier may wait, negotiate an outside-corridor swap, or pay the longer battery/time cost of the next tidal bypass. Another case: a loaded courier with insufficient remaining battery can finish a route after a neighbor transfers energy; without that transfer, it must reach a charger, change route or miss the deadline. The appropriate move changes because partner actions change state, not because an unrelated scalar interaction score is added.

Own timely delivery earns the main reward, while each delivery adds a small benefit to every robot and each expiry harms every robot. Private deadlines, batteries, queue positions and service credits can conflict with helping another courier. Cooperation can clear congestion or rescue an energy-starved courier, but a promise can also waste a turn or sacrifice a useful reservation. Plausible strategies include alternating reservations, negotiated swaps, energy relays, and timing a longer unreserved bypass. None has been learned, optimized or measured here.

## World, action order and actual commitments

candidate.py:24-77 initializes four physical robots, eight parcels, four dock chargers and three bridge cells. First parcels are released at time zero, second parcels at 36; deadlines are 55 and 115. A waiting own parcel within distance one can be picked up, moves with its actual carrier, and can only be unloaded at its exact target. A courier carries at most one parcel. A missed deadline expires the real waiting/carried parcel and clears the carrier link.

Actions 0–4 wait/move north/east/south/west; 5 picks/unloads; 6 requests a bonded lease; 7 yields; 8 charges; 9 transfers energy; 10 offers a swap. Availability is in candidate.py:109-125 and candidate.py:99-107. Invalid or unavailable actions become wait. One successful ordinary move costs one battery empty or two loaded. Failed contention does not consume movement energy. Concurrent proposals to the same initially empty target use rotating priority; ordinary movement never enters another robot's currently occupied cell.

In candidate.py:131-256, movement resolves first, then commitment timers age and bilateral swaps resolve. Inventory/queue/pledge/charging/transfer operations use rotating order. Crossing completion and credit compensation resolve before lease expiry and new arbitration. A new grant affects the next observation and move masks. After parcel following, the clock advances, new orders release, overdue parcels expire, and bridge schedules update. Same-step help does not retroactively legalize an action that was unavailable in the original mask.

A grant provides eight remaining steps. A pending request lasts ten; oldest age is favored after eligibility, not a monetary auction. A successful central crossing refunds exactly the posted bond. A yield commitment stores its own rewarded flag so the same pledge cannot earn unlimited compensation. Credits are capped at eight; deliveries add one service credit. These integer accounts are part of game resources, not reward labels.

The top bridge opens during public phase 0–3 and bottom during phase 6–9 of a twelve-step cycle. An occupied bridge cell remains open to let its occupant clear. A central corridor already containing a robot also remains physically open, while entry restrictions continue to protect existing traffic. The schedule and physical map are updated together by candidate.py:12-22.

Each parcel delivered at time t gives its owner `1 + 0.5 * max(0, (deadline-t)/deadline)`, plus 0.1 to everyone per delivery. Expiry costs its owner 0.5 and everyone 0.1 per expired parcel. Carrying costs 0.002 reward per step. Credits, swaps and energy transfers change actual resources; they do not award invented cooperation points. Conservative reward bounds [-3,3] are interface limits, not success or learning gates.

## End conditions, invariants and untested witness

The episode naturally finishes when all eight parcels are either delivered or expired; mixed delivery/expiry outcomes are preserved. Success means all eight are delivered. If no natural ending occurs first, 128 steps truncates, with natural priority on a simultaneous boundary. Every nonterminal raw call increments time once. candidate.py:258-259 dispatches terminal inputs to candidate.py:127-129, preserving all state, observation, clock and terminal cause with zero reward and no raw reset.

Declared invariants include in-bounds, distinct robot positions; battery [0,24]; credits [0,8]; at most one actual parcel per carrier; parcel status consistent with its carrier link; request age/TTL bounded by ten; lease TTL by eight; yield TTL by six; at most two completed parcels per robot; and charger capacity one served robot per transition. Energy transfers and swaps must conserve the stated energy amounts; no future replay may infer battery gain from a visual assistance effect alone. These dynamic cross-field assertions remain for actual sealed checking.

`playthrough.json` contains 35 manually reasoned steps intending first-batch pickup, opposed requests, partner yielding, sequential real crossings, compensation, a bilateral swap, two energy transfers, charging and first-batch deliveries. It intentionally ends before the second release and is not a full-episode success claim. No source execution, mask replay, timer calculation from a running environment, browser check or training took place in this author task.

Later paired action interventions should hold state/key/other actions fixed and compare yield versus retain-claim, request versus take-bypass, partner swap consent versus wait, and transfer versus conserve battery. Inspect partner route masks, lease timing, credit/resource ledgers, battery-enabled moves, actual carried trajectories and deadline outcomes over several steps. Distinct robot graphics without these consequences would not satisfy the mechanism.

Prospective variants could change lease duration, enforceability, queue rights, bridge timing, private deadlines, observation range or compensation conditions. Those would change the interaction relationships learned across games; none is implemented or empirically evaluated here. Game count, valid animation or interface acceptance cannot prove large-scale meta-RL adaptation.
