# Switchback Relay — authored tactical game, not executed

Status AUTHORED_NOT_EXECUTED. Three roles, 13 by 9 grid, 13 homogeneous discrete actions, 414 float32 observations, horizon 160. This is an original tactical extraction transition system, distinct from the manufacturing and warmth accounting of game 78. All signed integer world quantities are represented as int32. The reset/step key is accepted but deterministic rules do not consume it or duplicate it into state.

## Who changes whose possibilities

Two relay consoles at (4,2) and (4,6) maintain a central gate at (6,4). A nearby interacting crew member adds two charge, or three for the engineer, up to six. An unattended console loses one charge per step. Both charges must remain positive for the gate to open; an agent or core already on the gate cell holds it safely open until it clears. Consequently a partner's decision to stop channeling can remove the raider's retreat path several turns later. Charge buffers permit staggered shifts and short unattended windows instead of requiring one fixed pair of simultaneous button presses. Physical map passability changes; a gate icon alone is not the mechanic. See candidate.py:168-310 and candidate.py:131-136.

The scout spends one shared support point to mark turrets within distance six for eight turns. A marked turret's position, armor, firing countdown, aim and jam state become visible to every role. Marking also changes material damage: a hit does two armor damage while marked; an unmarked long-distance hit does zero. Unmarked close-range fire, launched within distance two of the target, does one. Thus the scout alters both partners' information and the payoff to an already available shot. The marked armor timer can expire during bolt flight. An ally who delays firing may lose the opportunity despite having seen an earlier scan.

The bulwark spends one support point to place a four-turn, one-cell shield in its facing direction. Enemy bolts physically stop on that cell. Any noncarrier can guard itself for one transition; a guarding bulwark also protects adjacent teammates. The engineer spends two support points to pause active turrets within distance five for four turns. Shield, scan and jam share the same pool: one agent's spending can prevent another's support action. Six finite base supply units can each replenish one own ammunition and one shared support point. This pool is not an independent per-agent counter.

A core carrier cannot fire, use a support ability or guard. Moving the core away from its initial bay also removes its old projectile-absorbing position. Dropping and an adjacent partner's later pickup can transfer this burden. Incoming bolts hit a squad body before a colocated carried core; an exposed dropped core loses hull and can terminate everyone's mission. Carrier survival, escort position and retreat timing therefore depend on partners' concrete actions.

Concrete partner-dependent response: with a scout mark active, a teammate can fire from behind the gate for meaningful armor damage; if the scout spends the last support point on a later scan too far away, the same distant shot may do zero damage and a close flanking move becomes a plausible alternative. Another case: a raider who expects both partners to continue channeling can retrieve directly; if one partner leaves, the sensible response can change to returning before charge expires, handing off the core, or asking the other partner to sustain the missing relay. These are hypotheses about the rules, not measured rational policies.

Plausible strategies include holding both relays while one marked-fire raider clears a lane; saturating charge then rotating an operator into escort duty; or making a quick pickup while partners maintain a retreat window. Shared support can be spent on threat interruption, armor exposure or physical shielding. No optimal strategy, training gain, measured strategic diversity, meta-learning transfer or non-isomorphism is claimed.

## Actual tactical objects and temporal resolution

candidate.py:24-96 defines the public terrain, cover, three turret emitters, relay consoles, extraction cell, finite supply dock and physical core. The public map has a vertical divider with exactly one dynamic gate. Turret cells block squad movement while alive. Cover and closed gates stop bolts as well as movement; open space does not. An empty movement target cannot be claimed by two live squad members, and initially occupied cells cannot be entered even when the occupant proposes leaving.

Actions 0–4 wait/move north/east/south/west, 5–8 fire in the corresponding cardinal directions, 9 uses the role-specific support, 10 channels a nearby console or otherwise picks/drops the core, 11 guards, and 12 refills at base. Console interaction has priority over core interaction. Invalid or unavailable actions become wait. Pre-step legality is in candidate.py:138-150; same-step contention may still consume a shared opportunity before a later actor, using rotating `(t+offset) mod 3` order.

Each nonterminal step resolves movement and carried-core movement; ages timers; resolves channels, pickup/drop, support and refills; changes gate passability; advances pre-existing bolts and resolves cover, shield, guard and damage; drops the core if its carrier dies; allocates new squad and enemy bolts; then updates milestones and terminal flags. Fresh bolts start at their actual emitter position and move from the next step. Fixed capacity is 24; a launch without a free slot does not consume ammunition, and a turret with no slot retries rather than inventing a projectile. Source of flight allocation is candidate.py:152-162.

Turrets acquire a cardinal aim when their countdown is two, preferring living high-noise or core-carrying targets, with distance and index ties. Firing/support/movement affect future noise. Aim then locks, so a visible windup supports anticipation and sidestepping; bolts never teleport or home. An unjammed turret counts down, fires, and waits six turns. Other turrets continue independently. A disabled turret no longer occupies movement space or fires, but its already launched bolts persist.

Shared reward is `-0.01` per transition, plus one-time gate opening 0.2, first core pickup 0.2, first crossing left of the gate 0.5, 0.5 per newly destroyed turret, minus 0.5 per newly lost squad member, and plus/minus 3 for extraction/failure. First-event flags prevent pickup/drop farming. Conservative bounds [-5,6] are mathematical interface limits, not performance acceptance thresholds.

## Terminal contract and checks still required

Success requires the core at (1,4), positive core hull and at least one living squad member. Core destruction or loss of the entire squad is natural failure; isolated dead roles retain the wait action while others continue. A nonterminal call increments the int32 clock once. Horizon 160 truncates only if no natural cause occurred. candidate.py:312-313 dispatches already-terminal inputs to candidate.py:164-166, preserving all state and terminal cause, with zero reward and no reset.

Documented invariants include in-bounds positions, unique live squad positions, health/ammunition nonnegative and bounded by four/five, support in [0,12], relay charge in [0,6], core hull in [0,6], finite supply [0,6], turret mark [0,8], jam/shield duration [0,4], and bolt TTL [0,14]. The machine manifest uses unambiguous nonnegative and uniqueness checks; cross-field gate/map, carrier/core and active/health consistency should also be checked later.

The 22-step playthrough is a manually reasoned hypothesis: two operators sustain the gate while a scouting raider scans, fires, sidesteps, retrieves, and attempts extraction. It has not been run; actual mask legality, impact timing, terminal time and success remain unknown. A genuine replay must report discrepancies. Useful future paired interventions hold state/key/all other actions fixed and compare channel versus leave over several turns, mark versus conserve before a partner shot lands, shield versus scan when a bolt approaches an ally, and pickup versus leave before the next hazard cycle. Inspect partner masks, visibility, damage, route availability and core location, not only total reward.

Rule variants might change relay leakage, range versus armor exposure, support sharing, carrier restrictions, cover and sight geometry or assignment of capabilities. These would change interaction requirements; no variant or final-test use is part of this authored train source. Rendering and interface acceptance cannot establish large-scale meta-RL learning.
