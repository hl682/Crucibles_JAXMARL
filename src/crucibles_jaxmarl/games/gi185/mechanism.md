# Mosaic Courtyard — original source-only mechanism

Status: AUTHORED_NOT_EXECUTED. The rules and script expectations below come from literal source inspection only. No candidate import/function call, JAX/NumPy call, rollout, seed search, rendering or training occurred.

Three builders move around a 7 × 7 map. The outer wall is impassable. An inner ring is ordinary walkway; the 3 × 3 courtyard at x,y in 2..4 is void until a physical tile is installed. Builders start at (1,2), (3,1), (5,4), facing east, south and west. The shared depot is (1,3). Nine uniquely indexed tiles exist for the entire episode: the first three are carried, and six share the depot. A builder carries at most one tile.

Tile patterns are straight [N,S], corner [N,E], tee [N,E,S], and cross [N,E,S,W]. Orientation 0 is that base pattern; each quarter-turn rotates clockwise. The actual four-bit tile_ports state is recomputed from identity and orientation and directly governs both movement and local compatibility. Initial held tiles are a north-south straight, a southwest corner and a north-east-south tee. This finite arrangement is source-authored, not chosen from a seed search or generated by a solver.

Placement targets the cell directly in front of a builder. It requires a held tile, an empty courtyard cell, no actor in that cell, and exact edge equality with every already-placed orthogonal neighbor: an open edge must face an open edge, and a closed edge must face a closed edge. Missing neighbors impose no condition, so open stubs may point into void for later construction. Installed tiles cannot be lifted. This irreversible commitment can preserve or eliminate the compatibility of a peer's tile.

Action 8 spends one of the acting builder's three finite pins to rotate its held tile, or, if empty-handed, a placed tile directly in front. A placed tile cannot rotate while occupied, and its new ports must still match every placed neighbor. Rotating a held tile has no adjacency test yet. A cross can consume a pin while its symmetric port pattern remains unchanged; the orientation records the actual quarter-turn. No rule grants a free reset or refunds a pin.

Movement onto courtyard cells requires an installed tile with an incoming port facing the mover. Leaving a tile requires its outgoing port in the movement direction. Thus stepping between tiles requires reciprocal open ports; walking around the ordinary ring needs no tile. A closed side remains a genuine travel barrier even if neighboring closed sides match the construction rule. Actors cannot stand in void. Installed tiles and socket coordinates govern traversal and completion, rather than merely decorating a numerical score.

Movement first tests input-state occupancy and ports, then resolves competing empty destinations by cyclic actor priority. Swaps and same-transition following are disallowed. An admitted movement attempt updates facing even if contention prevents displacement; an illegal movement becomes wait and does not turn. Action 5 turns facing clockwise without moving. After movement, tile/claim actions resolve in cyclic order and recheck the fresh local board. Two placements that were separately legal against the old board may conflict after the earlier installation; the losing action spends no tile or pin. Adjacent simultaneous rotations likewise recheck all new constraints. This is a specified priority rule, not arbitrary collision repair.

Pickup at the depot selects the lowest-index remaining stock tile. Returning a held tile to the depot preserves its identity and orientation. Passing gives the same tile to an adjacent waiting empty builder; cyclic priority chooses a recipient and subsequent actors see the updated ownership. Stock and carried tiles remain real entities, located at the depot or their carrier. Tiles are never created, deleted or silently converted. Status is 0 stock, 1 carried, 2 installed, and exactly one of those states applies to each of the nine identities. Only installed tiles must occupy distinct courtyard cells; depot and carried coordinates intentionally can overlap their associated entities.

Three public commissioning sockets lie at (4,2), (4,4), (2,4). A builder must physically occupy one and use action 10; each builder may commission once, then remain active to help. Its private preferred socket gives 2 rather than 1, and every builder receives 0.2 per commissioning in that transition. There is no global connectivity solver, automatic route completion or opaque puzzle-success state. Natural termination requires all three builders to commission; H=128 retains stranded or unfinished constructions. The reward interval [0,3] contains the source-derived maximum 2.6.

All spatial and construction state is public, including actual N/E/S/W ports. Private preferences are independent reset-key samples 0..2, fixed for the episode and present only in their corresponding observation row. O=157 consists of 155 public fields, own preference and own identity. Scene attributes show tile identity, carrier, installed status, orientation and actual port bits; no private socket preference or realized private utility is exposed. Pins are actual consumable resources, and every visible placement/rotation/claim event binds a real per-step flag.

Possible partner-dependent approaches include creating a two-tile shared corridor before pursuing a socket, delivering a compatible orientation to a partner instead of spending another pin locally, choosing a different corner/tee sequence to branch toward two preferences, returning an unsuitable held tile, or permanently committing a side that obstructs a rival route. A tile can be locally compatible yet trap its user or block later completion; such failed constructions remain valid outcomes. Neither solvability nor optimality is assumed.
