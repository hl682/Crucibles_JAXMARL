# Harbor Barter and Complementary Cargo

Status: AUTHORED_NOT_EXECUTED. This original initial-blind train-source proposal has no executed trajectory, R0 acceptance, learned policy or community animation acceptance.

Three boats move on a public 9 by 7 harbor grid with a central pier, two shallow approaches and two shared delivery docks at (3,1) and (5,1). Each boat starts with two items of its own type and capacity two. The six item identities and types persist for the entire episode. At reset a key-driven permutation assigns one private missing type to each boat; it needs one item of each of the other two types. Thus aggregate demand is exactly two of every type, while a boat does not receive other boats' demand rows. The reset key is used directly and is not retained in state. Source: candidate.py:L12-L44.

Movement is simultaneous. Another active boat's old cell is unavailable even if it will leave; all requests to a shared destination fail. A completed boat leaves the collision world. The old dock reservation blocks another boat's attempted entry, but the movement mask remains a terrain/recovery mask: a denied lease entry is a legal attempted move with no displacement. Entering a shallow tile sets cooldown one, so the next transition cannot move; that stationary turn may still be used for communication, exchange or booking. There are no swaps or follow-through moves. Source: candidate.py:L76-L98, candidate.py:L110-L205, candidate.py:L128.

Actions five through seven advertise an actually held cargo type, action eight withdraws, and the offer lasts four ticks. Action nine names the next id modulo three; action ten names the previous id. An exchange occurs only if adjacent boats name each other in the same joint action, both old offers remain live, and both still own an item of their advertised type. The lowest-id matching item is exchanged in each direction. Every actor names only one partner, so mutually consenting pairs are disjoint; refusing or choosing someone else preserves ownership. Offers are cleared after trade. No exchange condition uses the private demand of the counterparty. Source: candidate.py:L130.

Old reservations govern movement and delivery. Afterwards reservations expire or are released by action thirteen, and nearby action-eleven requests are settled in cyclic priority (id minus old t modulo three). A newly booked dock lasts three transitions. A physically occupied free dock can be booked only by its occupant. A nearby requester selects the lowest-index dock; a live booking is not renewed by an attempted reserve. Reserving can hold a scarce opportunity while another boat approaches, but it consumes a turn and cannot forcibly evict an occupant. Source: candidate.py:L151.

Action twelve deposits the lowest-id owned item that is still privately demanded, only while at an accessible dock. The cargo's owner changes to minus one, its credited boat is written permanently, and its physical location remains the actual dock tile. There is no cargo spawning or deletion. A boat finishes after two demanded deposits, departs and retains only wait. Its remaining inventory is then empty because it started with two, exchanges are one-for-one, and two items were delivered. Source: candidate.py:L170, candidate.py:L46-L57, candidate.py:L76-L98.

The conservation obligations are six physical items at all times, exactly two items of each immutable type, one owner or one permanent credited delivery per item, and no boat inventory above two. One-for-one exchange preserves counts; delivery transfers a real item into the dock ledger without allowing later pickup. Delivered counts are at most one per demanded type. Offer timers are in [0,4], dock timers in [0,3], recovery in [0,1], and ownership/type ids are bounded signed integers. The manifest declares reconstructed totals and nonnegative counters; these are static source arguments pending independent execution.

Per-transition individual reward is six for its own delivery, three for newly finishing, and minus one while it was active at the transition start. The bound is [-1,8]. A departed boat has zero subsequent reward. The joint episode ends naturally when all three have finished, otherwise at horizon ninety; there is no implied guaranteed completion under arbitrary private demands and strategic withholding. Natural completion takes priority at the horizon. Terminal calls preserve the state and clock with zero reward and all dones true. Source: candidate.py:L110-L205, candidate.py:L106-L108.

Observation width is 3 public + 10 self + 63 terrain + 24 boat + 36 cargo + 10 dock = 146, integer-valued float32 in [-1,90]. Private demand and delivered-count vectors appear only in the owning row. Terrain and dock lease telemetry are public. Other boats, advertised offers and cargo ownership are visible within Manhattan three without occlusion; hidden dynamic payloads are minus one with explicit visibility flags. Rendering uses these same visibility masks and never publishes private demand as a boat attribute. Source: candidate.py:L64-L74, candidate.py:L46-L57.

Plausible untested strategies include early bilateral trades followed by staggered dock use, or holding a type and reserving a dock until a desirable partner is positioned. A boat's best response can depend on whether another accepts, withdraws, blocks an approach or books the desired dock. It can be beneficial to spend a shallow-tile recovery turn negotiating. Cooperation can enable all deliveries while timing and scarcity make individual delay costs conflict. These are hypotheses, not measured equilibrium, learning or transfer results.

The first literal script proposes two mutual trades; its actions do not depend on the sampled demands. The second stops after approaches, recovery, bookings and dock entry, without asserting that the private demand permits a particular unload. Paired branches isolate refusal and a booking decision. All are UNEXECUTED_AUTHOR_HYPOTHESIS. Later variants could alter dock topology, offer lifetime or the private matching distribution, with new source identities and appropriate conservation review.

Scene events contain only public dock owners, timers and the conserved cargo count. Trade events and delivery flags are present in audit state but do not leak through global role-view event overlays. Real cargo follows its boat or remains at its delivery tile. The declarative scene is not itself an adapter, replay or verified animation.
