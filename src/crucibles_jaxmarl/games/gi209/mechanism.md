Compost Commons

Three gardeners share one compost cart, two screens, six finite crop rows, three moving pests, one odor source, a sorting station and a finite well. A horizontal irrigation channel can be crossed only at three bridge tiles. The cart and carried screens follow their current carrier; native positions govern touching, loading, watering, applying, harvest and delivery. All nineteen actions are available to every role.

Hands hold exactly one compost portion, cart handle or screen, or a produce batch. A separate flask holds at most two water units. Pickup/towing requires free hands and no produce. Movement uses old worker occupancy, boundaries and real channel tiles; no swaps/following occur and destination conflicts rotate by time. Out-of-range or masked actions become wait. Shared resource claims use rotating actor order and recheck live availability. Cart and tool operations require local Manhattan distance one. A cart can be moved to the sorter to add raw organic material; the sorter is not a remote refill button.

The cart starts with three portions at maturity one and moisture two, and three further raw portions remain at the sorter. Natural maturation adds one per tick only at moisture two through four with nonempty contents. Turning adds two regardless of moisture and emits a stronger odor; several accepted turns can contribute in a tick. Maturity is capped at eight and quality becomes mature at six. Each fourth tick a nonempty pile loses one moisture. Watering uses one flask unit for two bounded moisture credits, up to six; overly wet piles lose natural maturation and can continue emitting odor. Adding a raw portion resets the whole cart's maturity to zero, changing the quality available to peers. Empty carts reset age. A scoop captures current quality into the carried item, which does not continue maturing in the worker's hands.

Applying immature compost adds two growth and two stress; mature compost adds three growth and one moisture without that stress. Only a growing nearby crop can receive it. Scooping dirties the actor's personal tool, preventing another scoop until cleaning at the well spends one flask unit. The well begins with eighteen units, and each worker begins with one flask unit. Crop watering also uses one unit for two moisture credits. This links delayed fertilizer value, urgent growth, labor, water and another household's opportunity to use the remaining pile.

At the end of a working tick, turning a nonempty pile emits strength five at the cart's current position. A raw or overly wet nonempty pile also emits strength three on every third tick. Otherwise the existing odor loses one strength and its real point drifts one cell horizontally on even ticks, clipped to world bounds. Wind alternates east/west every eight ticks. All pests target an active odor point; absent odor, each targets the nearest live crop, with crop-index ties. They choose one legal cardinal step or stay by minimum Manhattan target distance. Wind sets the east/west tie order. This is a bounded local movement rule, not hidden path planning; a pest can wait when geometry blocks progress.

Deployed screens repel candidate pest cells within one Manhattan unit and protect crops at that range even if a pest cannot move away. Carried screens have no protective effect. Pests may share a cell, but damage counts actual unprotected crop contacts. Each contact removes a moisture credit and adds stress. Live crops also lose moisture every fourth tick, and dry crops gain stress each tick. A wet live crop recovers one stress on every fourth tick. Growing moist crops gain one growth every second tick; six ripens them. Stress four kills a live crop, with death priority over ripening on the same tick. Each ripe patch yields two produce exactly once; there is no reseeding or invisible replacement harvest.

For each crop origin, harvested produce equals carried produce plus private stock plus community deliveries. Initially six organic portions equal sorter stock plus cart portions plus carried compost portions plus applied count. Screens/cart/pests keep fixed identities. Private storage rewards its carrier 0.5 per unit and the crop owner 0.2. Donation rewards every role 0.5 per unit. Accepted non-wait actions cost 0.01 and failure costs one. Full information includes the actual odor point, wind, pest movement and all ledgers, so public scene events add no privileged information.

Natural success is six donated units. The source counts two units for each still growing or ripe crop; if that potential plus carried and already donated produce is below six, failure is natural. Privately stored produce cannot return to the community task. The first script turns, waters and scoops the pile; the second moves a screen through physical cells. The paired hypothesis changes one gardener's turn action after a common wait. Three suffix transitions are chosen to expose maturation timing and odor-driven movement during the same action history, without asserting an executed pest effect, solvability or successful harvest.
