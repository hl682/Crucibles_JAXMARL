Echo Quarry

A=3; dimensions and complete leaf declarations are in manifest.json. The block quarry has a dividing wall, six drillable salvage cells, three distinct support sites, two mobile insulation screens and three owner depots. Positions drive obstruction, contact, vibration range and delivery. Every actor uses the same eleven actions.

Movement uses old worker occupancy and deployed-screen obstruction. No swaps or following into a newly vacated worker cell occur; competing destinations use rotating rank (role - old_t) modulo three. Out-of-range or masked actions become wait. Contested object interactions use rotating actor order and recheck current ownership. The lowest eligible ore or slab index is chosen within Manhattan distance one; pickup prefers loose ore over screens. A worker holds at most one object. Dropping uses the worker's cell and carrying updates the object's native coordinates.

Drilling requires empty hands and an embedded ore parcel within one cell. Two accumulated work units expose it and clear its map cell. Several workers may contribute in the same joint action; each drilled wall emits one two-unit pulse regardless of how many crews worked on it. Vibration reaches every slab within Manhattan distance four of that wall, ignoring intervening rock. Each deployed screen within distance one of a slab subtracts two pulse units. Carried screens do not insulate. A worker standing on the slab contributes one load, plus one when carrying ore. A free-handed brace targets one nearby intact slab and subtracts two stress.

For an intact slab the update is old stress + wave + load when wave is positive - one natural relaxation - two when braced, clipped to [0,8]. Collapse at eight creates two rubble units and a permanent collapsed flag. If empty, this blocks a passage and costs everyone without ending the episode; clearing rubble restores traversable floor but never restores the support. A worker or active parcel on the newly collapsed tile causes injury or parcel loss and natural failure. Carried lost ore is detached and the carrier slot cleared. Quiet waiting, bracing, carrying screens to new sites, deliberate work staggering and longer perimeter routes are plausible alternatives, with different peer effects and timing.

The initial west screen protects the slab under the support worker. One worker can remove it while another drills, making the same drilling action stress the peer's site. The paired hypothesis isolates this removal versus leaving the screen deployed, then repeats the drill and waits once to inspect persistence. The literal scripts request supported excavation/delivery and a physical screen relocation. Timings derive only from the two-unit drilling and stress rules; no script was executed.

Every ore identity is embedded, loose, carried, delivered or lost. Screen identities persist; no resource is spawned by rendering. Each owner has two tagged parcels. Delivery pays its owner one, carrier 0.2 and every role 0.1. Accepted non-wait actions cost 0.01; drilling costs another 0.03. Each new collapse costs everyone 0.5 and fatal failure costs two. Finite reward bounds [-6,6] are conservative. Success requires all six parcels delivered; failure is actual collapse injury/loss, with the cap handled separately. The scene is fully observable, including progress, current support loads and real rubble.
