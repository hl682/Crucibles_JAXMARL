# Echo Hunt and Distributed Local Sensing

Status: AUTHORED_NOT_EXECUTED. Original initial-blind train-source rules and kernel; no numerical capture/escape, R0 acceptance, training or animation result is asserted.

Four players inhabit a public 11 by 7 grid: scouts zero and one versus runners two and three. The runners are controlled agents with independent choices, not scripted prey. Walls shape movement through a central passage and around eastern obstacles. Two physical beacons and two escape hatches are public landmarks. The initial layout is deterministic; reset and step accept their key without consuming it. Source: candidate.py:L12-L47.

Scout zero senses Manhattan one plus a horizontal beam of length three; scout one senses Manhattan one plus a vertical beam of length three. Runners see Manhattan two. Teammates share exact state. The sensor convention is explicitly radio-like: it ignores wall occlusion. Ordinary non-adjacent scout sensing is blocked by runner smoke. Every exposed player record includes its actual visibility flag and masks hidden payloads with minus one. Source: candidate.py:L49-L66, candidate.py:L85-L98.

A scout's action five spends one of its three tokens, exposes a pulse origin for two frames, and examines its own directional footprint at the simultaneously resolved positions. A detected active runner receives a three-frame live tracking timer shared with both scouts, so the teammate can receive information that its own local sensor lacks. Runner action five instead spends a token and creates two-frame smoke at its origin; smoke cancels that runner's pulse track, including a concurrent pulse, while adjacency can still reveal it locally. This creates an information/action-budget tradeoff rather than an additional cosmetic marker. Source: candidate.py:L188, candidate.py:L49-L66, candidate.py:L134-L213.

A scout beside a beacon can activate it for four frames, consuming one of its three finite charges. Public beacon telemetry is available to both teams. An active beacon exposes runners within Manhattan two to both scouts and pierces smoke. A nearby runner choosing action six spends a token, deactivates the chosen beacon and jams reactivation for two frames. Jamming wins a simultaneous activation attempt, so the blocked scout activation consumes no beacon charge. If multiple scouts request the same beacon it consumes only one charge; actors select the lowest-index eligible nearby beacon. Activation uses time and shared charges, whereas a runner's jam also uses its finite personal resource. Source: candidate.py:L100-L122, candidate.py:L166.

Ordinary movement uses one cell; actions eight through eleven attempt a two-cell dash for one token. Each dash resolves two simultaneous substeps. A blocked first hop cancels its second hop. An active actor's occupied cell is unavailable during each substep even if that actor is moving out, and all actors targeting a common destination fail. There are no swaps or follow-through. Terrain legality for both dash cells is checked before spending its resource; a spatial collision still spends the committed token. Source: candidate.py:L145, candidate.py:L100-L122.

Pulse, smoke, dash, runner jam and scout tag set special-action cooldown two. Ordinary moves remain available during cooldown. A scout's action seven is an attempted tag when ready; it captures active runners within Manhattan one that are legitimately visible after movement and the signal/beacon phases. A runner's action seven is available only at an escape hatch. Capture takes precedence over a simultaneous escape. Captured or escaped runners are inactive, nonblocking and have only wait until joint termination. Teammates retain a shared reward stake even when one runner is inactive. Source: candidate.py:L197, candidate.py:L100-L122.

Each captured runner gives both scouts +6 and both runners -6; each escaped runner reverses that sign. At most two runners resolve per transition, giving exact bounds [-12,12] with no additional time or terminal bonus. The game ends naturally after both runners resolve, or truncates at 72 transitions. Unresolved runners at the horizon are not silently relabelled as escaped. Natural completion has priority at the horizon. Already-terminal calls return unchanged state/clock, zero rewards and all dones true. Source: candidate.py:L134-L213, candidate.py:L130-L132.

Observation width is 9 self + 2 public counts + 77 terrain + 32 player + 12 beacon + 20 signal + 6 exit + 2 scout-track = 160, integer-valued float32 in [-1,72]. Beacon status, exits and fixed terrain are public. Signal origins are always visible to their team and only locally within Manhattan two to the opposing team. The two tracking timers appear only in scout observation rows. Source: candidate.py:L85-L98, candidate.py:L68-L78.

All persistent state leaves are signed int32 or bool. Personal tokens and beacon charges never regenerate; positive-resource masks and one-cost action categories keep them nonnegative. Timers are bounded by their literal resets, capture/escape status is irreversible, and active agent positions remain unique under the movement rule. The manifest declares relevant nonnegativity and identity obligations. These source-derived arguments do not replace numerical eager/jit/vmap or multi-step paired-intervention testing.

Plausible untested scout strategies include spending an early pulse to coordinate an approach or travelling to a beacon to preserve personal dash tokens. Runners can split routes, conceal one runner while the other distracts, jam a beacon or keep a token for a late dash. Pulse value changes with the teammate's position and sensor direction; smoke and jamming change the opponents' information. These are proposed interactions and strategies, not evidence of learnability, competitive balance or transfer.

The literal eight-turn script proposes a pulse, cooldown-respecting approach/tag and a separate runner's route to an exit. Its source-derived intended capture and escape are explicitly unexecuted. Paired branches change one actor's pulse, smoke or tag choice; none contains manufactured states or numerical rewards. Later variants might rotate sensor axes, relocate beacons or alter token budgets, each requiring new identity and validation.

Scene events are restricted to public resolved counts and public beacon timers. Private pulse/track details and hidden capture flags are not additional global role-view overlays. Signal markers use real stored origins and lifetimes. The package contains declarative presentation bindings, not an executed replay, renderer or public adapter.
